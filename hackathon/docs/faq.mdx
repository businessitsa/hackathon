# Frequently asked questions

### Is the hackathon free?

Yes. Participation is completely free - there are no registration or entry
fees.

### Can I participate online?

Yes. The hackathon is an online competition - teams can collaborate remotely
or meet in person, organising their own schedule throughout the hackathon.
The top teams will pitch in person at the finale.

### Do I need previous Interledger experience?

No. Teams will be introduced to the Interledger Protocol, Open Payments,
Rafiki and the supporting tools through documentation, workshops and mentor
support sessions.

### Can I register without a team?

Yes. Indicate on the registration form that you are registering as an
individual and we will do our best to place you into a team.

### How many teams can take part?

To ensure a high quality experience for participants and judges, places are
limited to **15 teams**. Teams are accepted on a first come, first served
basis once registration has been reviewed - so
[register](./register.mdx) early.

### How large can a team be?

Teams should ideally include three to five participants, with at least one
person able to work on the technical implementation.

### Do I need to be a developer?

No. The hackathon is open to designers, product people, fintech
professionals, students, entrepreneurs and community innovators - teams are
encouraged to be multidisciplinary.

### What must we build?

A working or partially working proof of concept that addresses a focused part
of the spaza shop payments challenge and makes meaningful use of Interledger
or Open Payments technology. See [The Challenge](./challenge.mdx) and
[Submissions](./submissions.mdx).

### Can we start working before the event?

Work should primarily be completed during the official hackathon period. You
are welcome to research the problem space and learn the technology in
advance.

### Who owns the solution?

Teams retain ownership of their work.

### What happens if our prototype is incomplete?

That's okay - partially working prototypes may be submitted. Teams are not
judged purely on polish: a smaller, well-considered working solution may
perform better than an ambitious but incomplete idea. Be clear about known
limitations and recommended next steps in your submission.

### How will teams receive support?

All teams receive access to the challenge brief, technical documentation,
workshops, mentor check-ins and the event communication channels. See
[Technology & Resources](./technology.mdx) for the Discord community and
mentor support details.

### What happens at the Grand Finale?

The top five teams are invited to present their solutions live at the
in-person Grand Finale in Cape Town, where the winners are announced. Venue
arrangements, including catering and connectivity, will be confirmed closer
to the event - finalists should bring their own laptop and anything else they
need to present comfortably.

### When will the challenge brief be released?

The challenge brief will be shared with registered teams - the release date
will be announced on this site and in the event communication channels.
