# The Challenge

Spaza shops play an important role in South African communities, providing
convenient access to food, household goods and essential services.

However, many spaza shops still rely heavily on cash. Existing digital payment
options may involve high transaction costs, expensive hardware, complex
onboarding, delayed settlements or systems that do not work easily across
different payment providers.

This can make it difficult for small businesses to adopt digital payments
while maintaining control over their costs and cash flow.

## The numbers

The scale of the informal payment economy is significant:

- Approximately **150,000 spaza shops** operate nationally, contributing
  around **5.2% of GDP** and supporting roughly **2.6 million livelihoods**
- Informal retail accounts for around **30% of South Africa's total retail
  market**, and as much as **40–50% of grocery sales** in township communities
- **Nine out of ten transactions** in South Africa are still completed in
  cash, with cash dominance close to **90% in the informal sector**
- **R19.3 billion** was remitted from South Africa to SADC countries in 2024,
  at an average cost of around **12.7%** - over four times the G20 target of 3%

## The hackathon question

:::tip
**How might we use open and interoperable payment technology to make digital
payments more accessible, affordable and practical for spaza shops?**
:::

## Why open, interoperable payments?

The [Interledger Protocol](https://interledger.org/developers/get-started/)
(ILP) and [Open Payments](https://openpayments.dev/) are designed to
complement existing payment systems rather than compete with them. Three
properties make them a good fit for the South African problem:

- **Open and currency agnostic.** ILP transfers value between any pair of
  ledgers, including non-bank stores of value such as mobile money wallets,
  prepaid balances and fintech accounts. It can route from a bank account to a
  mobile wallet to a cross-border recipient on a different ledger entirely.
- **A clean developer primitive.** Open Payments wallet addresses work like
  URL-based public aliases for accounts - analogous to email addresses. Any
  Open Payments enabled application can request quotes, obtain user consent
  and initiate payments through a single, standard interface, without holding
  sensitive credentials or integrating with each bank individually.
- **Aligned with where local rails are heading.** South Africa's instant
  payment systems are built on ISO 20022 messaging standards, which converge
  with what ILP and Open Payments expect at the edges. Solutions built on Open
  Payments today are not orphaned from the direction of the local ecosystem.

In practice this means a shopper could pay any merchant who exposes a wallet
address - no point-of-sale device and no closed-loop scheme lock-in - and
low-value, peer-to-peer flows (such as stokvel contributions) become simple
programmable payments.

## What solutions might explore

- Low-cost digital payment acceptance
- Payments without expensive point-of-sale hardware
- Interoperability between different wallets or payment providers
- Faster or more transparent settlement
- Offline or low-connectivity payment experiences
- Simple onboarding for shop owners
- Better transaction records and payment visibility
- Customer loyalty or community-based payment features

Teams do not need to solve every issue. Choose a specific part of the problem
and address it well.

## How the hackathon works

The hackathon follows four stages, from understanding the problem to pitching
a working prototype:

- **Understand the challenge.** Review the problem brief and learn how spaza
  shops handle payments today - the costs, the friction and the workarounds.
  From there, identify one focused problem your team wants to address rather
  than trying to solve everything at once.
- **Define your solution.** Develop your concept: who your users are, the use
  cases you are targeting and the payment journey you propose. A clear,
  well-scoped design makes the build phase far more productive.
- **Build a proof of concept.** Use the Interledger and Open Payments
  technology, documentation and support sessions to create a functional or
  partially functional prototype. Workshops and mentors are available along
  the way - a focused prototype that works beats an ambitious one that
  doesn't.
- **Present your solution.** Submit your project and pitch it to the judging
  panel. Explain the problem you chose, your solution, the technical
  implementation and the potential impact for spaza shops and their
  communities.

See [what teams must submit](./submissions.mdx) and the
[judging criteria](./judging.mdx) for full details.
